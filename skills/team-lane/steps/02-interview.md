# Step 2 — Interview the user

**Interview the user, the Socratic way: do not tell, ask — and ask the
   question that points straight at the hole in what you know.** **One question per
   turn**, each with your recommended answer. Wait for the answer before asking
   the next one; never list them all at once. Look up every fact you can in the
   repository instead of asking. Why: principle 22 in principles.md.

   **Six kinds of question.** Work out which kind of thing you are missing
   first, then pick the kind of question that opens it.

   1. **Clarify** — what those words actually point at.
   2. **Probe an assumption** — what is believed without being checked.
   3. **Reason and evidence** — whether the judgement rests on anything.
   4. **Another view** — the option nobody has put on the table yet.
   5. **Consequence** — what this choice drags in behind it.
   6. **Question the question** — whether this is the problem to solve at all.

   **Use kind 6 early.**

   **Ask wide first, narrow later.**

   **Two ways this goes wrong.**

   - **A leading question** hides the answer you want inside the question — "you
     need it to be fast, right?". If you feel you already know the answer, **go
     and look it up**: the code, the files they gave you, what they have already
     said. Never put a question mark on your own guess.
   - **Making the user feel tested.** No score, nobody caught out. The two of you
     look at the problem together, not at each other.

   **Judge every question for whether it can be skipped.** A question can be
   skipped **only when its answer changes neither what gets built nor what gets
   released** — being able to start the work without the answer is not enough on
   its own. For a question that can be skipped, offer a **"leave it undecided"**
   option beside your recommended answer. **A question that cannot be skipped gets
   no such option**, and you say in one line why it cannot.

   **When to stop.** Stop at the moment you could write down every section of
   the opening document with no guess left in it. Not one question sooner, not
   one question later. The one thing that is never right is asking a question you
   already have the answer to.
